Fuzzy Hybrid Model for Stock Trading

In this stock-trading research project, I reduced the predictor's inputs from 96 values to 26, then combined a deep-learning price predictor with fuzzy buy, sell, or hold rules.

GitHub

What it is

Coursework project with Tristan Till. The system predicts next-day stock direction, then uses a fuzzy trading head to convert market signals into buy, sell, or hold actions for a simulated portfolio.

Primary reported stock: Exxon Mobil (XOM). Market data came from Yahoo Finance, with a chronological train / validation / test split.

My contribution: feature-space redesign

Early versions fed the sequence model a broad input table: 16 engineered columns across 6 market series, producing 96 backbone input channels.

Through domain analysis and feature engineering, I redesigned that into a smaller, more targeted representation that shipped in the final system:

  • 13 engineered features per ticker, emphasizing relative price and volume changes and multi-scale momentum / trend signals (including RSI and volume-aware RSI across short, medium, and longer windows, plus ROC, MACD, and a volume MACD signal)
  • Target stock + S&P 500 only
  • 26 backbone input channels total

Exact change: 96 → 26 channels (~72.9% fewer). The redesign replaced a broad, weakly focused representation with a smaller set of targeted market and momentum signals.

Why the redesign mattered

The predictor received far fewer channels at each timestep, reducing input complexity. The final system also used a much smaller recurrent configuration than the early oversized stack. Together, that made training and iteration substantially lighter. No exact runtime multiplier is claimed.

How the full system worked

At a high level:

26-channel market representation → GRU-LSTM-Attention predictor → next-day price-change signal → fuzzy trading head → buy / sell / hold

The predictor reads a 60-day window of the 26 channels and estimates next-day price change.

The trading head is separate. It does not take the full sequence tensor. It receives a compact set of 8 scalars (momentum and trend-style signals for the target and S&P 500, the predictor’s output, and portfolio liquidity).

Instead of hard thresholds, the fuzzy head maps continuous signals into degrees such as low, medium, and high, then combines them through learned rules. Each of the 8 inputs is scored by three triangular membership functions (24 membership grades). Three objective-specific fuzzy heads (return, risk-adjusted return, and drawdown) vote by majority for the final action.

96 → 26 is the backbone input reduction. 8 → 24 membership grades is a later fuzzy expansion. They are different stages.

Results

Directional hit rate means how often the predictor got the next-day up/down direction correct.

Exxon Mobil (final reported system):

  • Reported baselines (zero-change / LSTM): 47.74%
  • Final GRU-LSTM-Attention system: 61.29%
  • Difference: +13.55 percentage points

These are final-system results. The redesigned representation is what that system trained on; the repository does not preserve a controlled old-96-channel versus new-26-channel comparison proving the feature redesign alone caused the entire gain.

Transfer to Chevron

The backbone was fine-tuned for Chevron (CVX) while the fuzzy logic was retained, as a check beyond Exxon. Directional performance there did not beat the LSTM baseline (hybrid 53.55% vs LSTM 56.13%). Transfer is supporting context, not the headline.

What the experiments suggested

Ablations suggested the fuzzy trading head relied meaningfully on engineered market signals rather than simply mirroring day-ahead prediction quality.

Technical implementation

Python and PyTorch for the sequence model; a custom genetically trained fuzzy inference system (GenFIS) for the trading head; Yahoo Finance market data; chronological train / validation / test split.

Team attribution

Coursework project with Tristan Till. My contribution focused on the feature-space redesign that became the shipped backbone input representation.
